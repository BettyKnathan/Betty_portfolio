My career began in operations, not in learning. As a fraud analyst I learned the importance of judgement, customer impact and understanding the work itself. An early leadership development opportunity sparked my curiosity about how thoughtfully designed development can prepare people for roles they have not yet held.

At IBM, I moved from customer registration and escalation leadership into learning, bringing operational understanding with me. Across subsequent roles in investment-management client enablement, digital learning, custody banking, sales enablement, change and capability consulting, that instinct remained consistent: understand the business, understand the people doing the work, diagnose what is getting in the way, and build practical ways forward.

Today, hands-on experimentation with AI is extending that curiosity into a new set of questions. I am deliberately building deeper capability in AI governance, AI risk and resilience, connecting emerging technology with foundations shaped by BFSI, risk and controls, change and human capability.
